# JedidiahOps Context

Compact domain map for implementation and planning. Search this file first for names and invariants; read only the sections relevant to the work.

## Core Model

**Job** is one production-floor work record. Do not call it an Order, Work Order, Build, or Ticket. A **Product Job** is bound to one **Product Unit** and has a `JOB-xxxxx` code; it is either a **Build Job**, which creates that Unit, or a **Rework Job**, which fits further Assemblies to a Unit that already exists. A **Custom Job** comes from a draft, sent, or accepted Custom Quote, has no Product Unit, and uses its Quote's Work Title as its display name. Every Job has a Product Unit, a Quote, or both: a Custom Job always has a Quote, a **Stock Build** has only a Unit, and build-to-order and Rework Jobs have both. A Job also carries a freeform **Description**. A Job displays the Owner of its Product Unit, or its Quote's Customer when it has no Unit; a Job on an unowned Unit reads as **Stock**. A **Cancelled Job** (its `cancelledAt` is set) is produced only by cancelling its Quote — there is no direct Job cancellation. It is hidden from every live list, search, and picker, and all Job mutations reject it, with one carve-out: its remaining Slots may still be removed manually. Its **retained** `done` and `active` Slots stay on the Board and Calendar as read-only history (styled Cancelled); the Job sheet opens read-only. Cancellation is terminal — there is no un-cancel.

**Job Completion** is the Job's stored `completedOn`: the plant business date it finished, or `null` while open. It **latches** — once set, nothing rewrites or clears it automatically, so a resized upstream Slot can never make a recorded completion date drift. A human sets or clears it on the Job sheet (`job:update`, no future dates); the nightly **completion sweep** otherwise stamps every open, non-cancelled Job whose Work Slots are all `done` with that Job's latest `lastWorkDay`. The sweep is strictly additive (`NULL` → date only), so backfilling history is just its first run, and it audits each write with a null (system) actor — the audit log is the only record of a date's origin. A Job with no Work Slot is never swept; a Cancelled Job is abandoned, not completed. Keep this distinct from **schedule completeness** (every Work Slot `done` against plant today), which stays derived, reacts to Slot changes instantly, and alone owns Active Board windowing and the Book Slot picker. The Job List and the inventory close-out queue read `completedOn`.

**Product Unit** is one physical machine. It owns the **Product Serial Number**, the Product it was built as, and an optional **VIN Number**. It is created with its Build Job and never on its own; a Custom Job produces none. Its **As-Built Spec** is the union of its Jobs' Build Specs, counting only live Jobs — a Cancelled Job's CFO is a plan, not a record of what was fitted. A Unit is **In Build** until its Build Job has a Job Completion and **On Hand** from then on, both derived from that Job and never stored; a Cancelled Job is skipped, so a rebuilt Unit reads from the build that actually ran. A Unit that is On Hand and owned by a Customer reads as **Complete**: a label over the build state and the Owner together, applied at the surface rather than a third state of the machine, so the Units list offers In Build, On Hand, and Complete as filters and its On Hand narrows to the ones we still hold. Avoid Machine, Item, Asset, or Stock Item.

**Unit Ownership** is an append-only log of **Ownership Transfers** on one Product Unit: from Customer (null means we hold it), to Customer, the date it happened, an optional sourcing Quote, and the actor. The current **Owner** is the newest Transfer's destination and is never a column; a Unit with no Transfers is **Stock**, meaning we hold it. Owner is the Customer our records attribute the machine to — not legal title, payment, or delivery — so a build-to-order Unit is owned by its buyer while it is still in a Bay. A Transfer is written when a build-to-order Unit is created, when an Allocation Quote is accepted, and by hand for a resale between Customers or a return to Stock. Cancelling a Quote that moved ownership writes a reversing Transfer rather than erasing the round trip.

**Build Spec** is a Job's own selection of Optional Assemblies and the only source a CFO is snapshotted from. A Quote's selected Assemblies *seed* it at Job creation the way the Work Item Rate Card seeds a rate; a Stock Build enters it directly. A Rework Job's Build Spec is only the Assemblies being added, so its CFO covers that work alone.

**Stock Build** is a Job with a Build Spec and no Quote. It builds a Product Unit we hold, with no Customer and no commercial record until the Unit sells. There is no Customer record standing in for the company itself.

**Quote** is a sales offer for one **Customer**, owned by one salesperson. A **Product Quote** references one Product, can source at most one Job once accepted, and becomes a **Locked Quote** once it sources a Job: commercial facts stay frozen, while post-sale logistics and notes can still change where the UI allows. An **Allocation Quote** is a Product Quote that also references an existing Product Unit, selling a machine we already hold instead of one to be built: its selected Optional Assemblies are seeded from the Unit's As-Built Spec and all charge as if built to order, it locks on acceptance rather than on sourcing a Job, and the only Job it can source is a Rework Job. Accepting one transfers ownership and is refused unless the Unit is Stock at that moment, so any number of draft or sent Quotes may compete for one Unit but only one can close. **Invoice Number** is the Quote's record of the sale's invoice; it stays editable while locked, because a stock sale needing no work never sources a Job. A **Custom Quote** has no Product and no base price (it is forced to zero); it is priced entirely from a Work Title and its Work Items. It can source at most one Custom Job while draft, sent, or accepted, and locks commercial facts once accepted except that its Work Items remain editable. A Quote can be **Cancelled** into a terminal, irreversible status that also locks it: an unlocked Quote via the status dropdown (`quote:update`), a Locked Quote via an admin-only destructive flow (`quote:cancel`). Cancelling a Quote that has sourced a Job cascades to **cancel that Job** and remove its future (`scheduled`) Slots in one transaction. The remedy for a mistaken cancellation is a new Quote.

**Customer** means the company buying or receiving the product. Avoid Client or Buyer in code and docs.

**Product Range** is an admin-managed catalog grouping intended for Product marketing and selection. Every Product belongs to exactly one Range. A Range carries a name and an optional presentation image for customer-facing documents. Avoid Category, Line, or Family. Range management (create, update, and the management list) is admin-only; Product forms load Range options through Product access.

**Product Range Variant** is an admin-managed, ordered sub-grouping within one Range, carrying only a name. A Product optionally belongs to exactly one Variant, which must be a Variant of its Range; changing a Product's Range clears its Variant. A Range may have no Variants. Variant names are unique within their Range, not globally. A Variant cannot be removed while any Product references it. Variants exist for marketing and selection refinement. Avoid Model, Trim, Sub-range, or Option (Option already means Optional Assembly selection on Quotes). Variant management lives with Range management and is admin-only; Product forms load Variant options through Product access.

**Supplier** is currently a standalone procurement directory record. It cannot be removed while it has a draft Purchase Order, which is still editable and would be stranded; sent and cancelled orders keep their Supplier as history. **Part** is the reusable stockable catalog item, bought or built. A Part's Unit of Measure is its counting unit everywhere. A linear Part uses `mm` and must have a positive **Standard Purchase Length** in millimetres; `mm` marks the class its lengths are measured in, never what its quantities count — linear stock is always counted in whole pieces, and metres are display formatting of `mm` rather than a second unit. **Storage Location** is findability-only, never a stock-ledger dimension. A Part's Unit of Measure and its Supplier both freeze once the Part appears on a Purchase Order that was not cancelled, since either would silently rewrite what that order asked for. A Part has **either a Supplier or a BOM — never both, never neither**: a **Built Part** carries a Bill of Materials instead of a Supplier and is made in-house, so it can never sit on a Purchase Order line. Everything else about it is unchanged — it is stocked, checked out, costed, labelled, and specced into a CFO exactly like a bought Part. Its BOM may be empty, which is the trivial build of a Part whose components are all raw material. **Assembly** is a Product-owned grouping of Parts, either Standard or Optional; an Assembly's kind is immutable after creation. Optional Assemblies carry an upgrade-delta price, may replace whole Standard Assemblies, and are selected on Product Quotes.

A **Part Label** is an on-demand printable identity for exactly one Part: Code 128 encodes only the Part code, while readable text repeats its code, name, and Storage Location. Labels are never stored or tracked, are never scoped by Supplier, and linear labels stay per Part rather than per length bucket.

## Stock

A **Stock Movement** is one append-only, actor-attributed ledger fact that changes a Part's stock or cost; it is never edited or deleted. Stock on hand is the sum of movement deltas per Part, bucketed by length for linear Parts. A **Checkout** draws a Part from stock against a Job at the current moving average; a **Return to Store** reverses it at the cost still drawn by that Job, and stays available after the Job is cancelled so recovered stock is not stranded off-ledger. A **Receipt** is the arrival of stock against one open Purchase Order line — it attaches to that line, defaults its cost from the line's price and its length from the Part's standard purchase length, and is the ledger write receiving *is*, never a paper step posted later. A **Return to Supplier** sends received stock back off the line it arrived on, at that line's stamped receipt cost and under a reason (`wrong-item`, `defective`, `order-error`) — value-neutral for the Part, since the quantity leaving is worth what it was worth arriving. It needs a Receipt behind it, stays available after an order is closed short, and is the one consumption-shaped movement a periodic Part accepts, because it reverses an arrival rather than recording use. Its reason decides whether the line re-opens: `wrong-item` and `defective` say a replacement is owed, so the return nets off what the line has received and the order waits on it again — On Order included — while `order-error` says nothing is coming in its place and leaves the line as received. Both Job movements are valid off-CFO, and every movement warns rather than blocks when quantities disagree: a draw past the CFO, a draw taking a length bucket negative, a return past what the Job still holds, a receipt past what the line ordered, and a return to the Supplier past what the line received are all warnings, judged once and shown both before the post and on it. A **Build** is the production event that turns a Built Part's components into finished units: one transaction, value-preserving, consuming each component at its stamped moving average and producing a single row at consumed value ÷ units built — or at no cost at all when nothing consumed carried one. Consumption prefills at BOM × N and is edited to what actually left the rack; deviating from the BOM warns, and a short rack goes negative, because the build already happened. BOMs nest but **builds never recurse**: components come from stock, so inner batches are built to the rack before outer builds consume them, and raw material posts no consumption at all. **Commitment** is derived per Job and Part as `max(0, CFO quantity − drawn quantity)` until inventory close-out; **Free Stock** is stock on hand minus the sum of open commitments. **Close-out** is the inventory-local assertion that a Job's stock life has ended: recorded once against a completed, live Job, it releases that Job's remaining commitment permanently — later movements change what is drawn but never re-open the released demand. It is not a Job status, and reopening is not a concept; cancellation releases commitment its own way and is never closed out. The **close-out queue** is every completed Job still holding drawn stock or open commitment, ordered oldest first — a Job leaves it by being closed out, or by having nothing outstanding left to close, and the days it has waited are the stale-commitment signal.

**On Order** is the sum of `ordered − received` over the open lines of Sent Purchase Orders that have not been closed short — the cover the shelf does not hold yet. It is always shown *beside* Free Stock and never folded into it. **Minimum Stock** is an optional per-Part reorder level, edited on the Part like any other attribute. The **buy list** is every Part carrying at least one of three tagged reasons — Free Stock negative, stock on hand below Minimum Stock, or stock on hand at or below zero — ranked by the earliest unfinished Work Slot date among the Jobs whose commitment drives it, with Parts no scheduled Job is waiting on ranked last. Its **suggested buy** is the largest of those shortfalls less On Order, floored at zero. Nothing on the buy list ever raises an order by itself: a ticked selection splits into one Draft Purchase Order per Supplier, unpriced, and a Built Part can never be ticked because it has no Supplier to buy it from. A **Late Purchase Order** is a Sent order past its expected delivery date, not closed short, with lines still owed. **Returns awaiting credit** is every Return to Supplier movement no credit note references — money the plant is still owed, keyed per movement so a partly credited order keeps showing the rest.

**Stock Tracking Mode** is per Part: perpetual records every stock movement, while periodic records only its opening balance, its receipts, and its stocktake counts — a periodic Part rejects consumption movements and every other adjustment reason outright, because between counts its stock on hand is deliberately stale-high and only a count can correct it. Receipts are deliberately *not* among what it rejects: bought-in raw material still arrives on a Purchase Order, and the receipt is the baseline the next count corrects.

A Part's moving average cost is derived from ordered cost-bearing movements, is per millimetre for linear Parts, and remains absent — “no cost yet,” never zero — until a cost is established. Internally fabricated Parts are the deliberate exception: they carry zero material cost because their raw material is charged separately. Costs are gated: a caller without inventory cost access reads every cost field as null, on every surface that carries one.

**Work Item** is a Custom Quote charge describing one unit of custom work. Every Work Item carries an optional description, a labour duration in hours (fractional, zero allowed), the ex-VAT **hourly rate** it was priced at, and zero or more Work Item Parts. It is named one of two ways: a **departmental Work Item** takes its name from its Department and stores none, while the department-less **Other** Work Item is the only shape that carries its own name. Its total is hours × its own rate plus the sum of its Parts, rounded to cents; it contributes to the discountable ex-VAT subtotal. Labour-only, parts-only, and zero-cost Work Items are all legal, as is a Custom Quote with no Work Items. Work Items are an explicit commercial-fact exception to Quote locking and remain editable after acceptance and after a Custom Job starts. The Custom Job displays its Quote's Work Items by name only; the customer-facing Quote Document prints each Work Item as name + optional description + total, with a positive-duration Labour row (hours + hourly rate + subtotal) followed by its Parts (quantity + name + unit price + subtotal). Zero-hour Work Items omit the Labour row.

**Work Item Rate Card** is the default ex-VAT hourly rate per Department, held in `@pkg/domain` (`WORK_ITEM_DEPARTMENT_RATES`). It only *seeds* a Work Item: each row snapshots the rate it was priced at, so editing the card never reprices an existing Quote, and one Quote may mix Departments at different rates. A Department becomes quotable by gaining a rate on the card — the database check already permits the full Department enum, so no migration is involved. Unrated Departments seed at zero. There is no Quote-level hourly rate.

**Work Item Part** is a freeform row on one Work Item — required name, positive whole-number quantity, non-negative unit price. It is *not* a catalog Part and never references one; the resemblance is sales-facing only.

**Quote Pricing** is the computed breakdown — ex-VAT subtotal, VAT amount/rate, VAT-inclusive customer total, discount amount, Product Quote live selected Optional Assemblies, and Work Items (Custom Quotes) — projected from a Quote's stored pricing facts. Product Quotes latch base price/currency from Product at creation; Custom Quotes hold a zero base price, price entirely from Work Items, and use `ZAR`. A selection is excluded when stale. On persisted Product Quotes a `null` reference is the complete stale set (Assembly kind is immutable and deletion is `on delete set null`); while editing, in-flight selections are additionally resolved against the freshly loaded catalog. Live selections list in catalog display order on every surface. Deposit (a payment term) is not an input. Per-Quote price surfaces use the VAT-inclusive total; reporting aggregates explicitly labelled ex-VAT use the subtotal.

**CFO** means Customer Fabrication Order: the Job's frozen bill of materials, snapshotted from the Job's Build Spec at Job creation.

## Manufacturing

The visual pipeline is fixed: Procurement -> Supply -> Fabrication -> Paint -> Assembly. A **Department** groups **Bays**, but is not an authorization boundary.

**Bay** is a durable physical workspace. Avoid Station, Workstation, Cell, or Machine. Bays are created, renamed, disabled, and re-enabled by admins. Disabled Bays reject new bookings but keep existing schedule history.

**Bay Operator** is a permissionless shop-floor user role. An **Operator Assignment** attaches one Bay Operator to one Bay until unassigned; the current operator is the open assignment interval, not a column on the Bay.

**Product Bay** is a Product's default Bay plus working-day duration. It seeds Job scheduling, but it is not required and does not derive from quote lead time.

## Scheduling

**Bay Queue** is the ordered Slot sequence for one Bay from its `scheduleOrigin`. All queue mutations serialize on the Bay row. Avoid using Schedule for this per-Bay sequence.

**Slot** is a whole-day planning block in a Bay Queue. Avoid Booking, Reservation, Appointment, or Block. A **Work Slot** references one Job. An **Idle Slot** reserves working time without a Job and is distinct from an Off-Day.

Slot dates are derived, never stored on Slots. Projection walks a Bay Queue from the Bay's plant business-date origin, consuming `durationDays` as working days and skipping Off-Days. Derived dates are `yyyy-MM-dd` plant business dates and must read the same in every viewer timezone. Africa/Johannesburg is used only at the server boundary to derive plant "today" and new Bay origins.

A Slot's span is half-open `[startDate, endDate)` and tiles its Bay Queue without gaps, so a span can open on an Off-Day. Projection therefore also emits **`firstWorkDay`** and **`lastWorkDay`** — the inclusive working days the Slot actually covers, snapped onto the Working Calendar. Geometry (Gantt bars, segments, day breakdowns) reads the raw span; every human-readable date label reads `firstWorkDay`/`lastWorkDay`, never raw span arithmetic.

**Working Calendar** is explicit dated facts. Unmarked dates are working days. **Off-Days** are explicit non-working dates. **Bay Calendar Exceptions** override the org calendar for one Bay: Overtime opens an otherwise-off day; Bay Closure closes an otherwise-working day. Off-Days are stepped over by projection and are not Idle Slots.

**Job Days-Left** is a Job-level horizon, distinct from a single Slot's remaining days: the maximum, over the Job's unfinished Work Slots, of the working days from plant "today" through that Slot's last work day. It answers when the Job is fully off the floor, paced by its last Bay, and counts the working days in any idle gap before a not-yet-started Slot.

**Insert at Date** is a placement hint, not a stored or pinned start. Under the Bay lock, the chosen date resolves against live projection: inside a Slot splits it, exactly at a Slot start inserts before it, and past the next available day appends. Deliberate deferral beyond the queue end is represented with Idle Slots.

**Board** is the full cross-Bay projected state: every Bay Queue projected against its Working Calendar, with each Slot's state (done, active, or scheduled against plant "today") and cross-Bay Job completion resolved. It is built in one pass from stored facts and never stored itself; every schedule read is a slice of the Board. Avoid Shop or Schedule for this whole.

**Active Board** is the default window over the Board, as read through `jobs.listBays`: every Slot of a Job that still has an unfinished Work Slot, unioned with Slots ending on or after a `from` floor (default plant "today"). Fully-complete Jobs — every Slot's projected `endDate` at or before today — drop off, bounding the shared read to work-in-progress plus forward bookings. The server projects the full queue and computes the cross-Bay Job-completion map before trimming; only the `slots` array is windowed, never `nextAvailableDate` or other queue-end scalars. The Gantt scrolls into history by lowering its own `from` (clamped to ~12 months back) on the same query. Projection-dependent previews (insert-seeds placement and ghost bars) resolve server-side via `jobs.previewSchedule`; the Working Calendar stays client-side. See ADR 0009.

## Commercial Documents

**Document** is a stored file an owner accumulates in a collection — it carries its own filename, owner-type-specific metadata, and identity. Its stored content and metadata are immutable, although an owner-specific policy may allow its collection row to be deleted. It is distinct from a single named file held directly as a field of an entity (such as a Product's hero or logo image), which is not a Document. Reserve Document for the collection member; avoid Attachment, Media, or Asset. File is not a domain object: it names the shared stored-file primitive (content-type agnostic) that both a Document and an entity's file fields are built on. Documents use private object storage through the API; deleting the row does not delete the stored object. Metadata is immutable and owner-type specific.

**Thumbnail** is separate: a small inline display image for quick recognition.

**Job Document Snapshot** copies the Product's current uploaded Documents onto a Product Job at Job creation, pointing to the same immutable stored files and freezing metadata. The Brochure is not copied but generated fresh and saved as its own Job Document. Custom Jobs start with an empty Documents collection and do not generate a Brochure. Product display names may still read live. Snapshot Documents and the saved Brochure cannot be deleted.

**Purchase Order** is the live procurement order placed with exactly one Supplier. It starts as a Draft whose expected delivery date, Part lines, and optional links to one or more Jobs are one editable aggregate saved together; an order with no linked Jobs is restock. A line records a quantity in the Part's purchasing unit and its agreed unit price, and every line's Part must belong to the order's Supplier. A line's price is a stored fact, so only the API's cost gate can hide it — which also means editing a draft needs inventory cost access, not just Purchase Order rights. Marking it Sent is a human assertion that freezes editing and saves one immutable, Purchase-Order-owned PDF containing the order number and linked Job codes. That same Document is projected into every linked Job's Documents tab without copying its bytes or ownership, and because it prints prices it is visible there only to cost readers. Only a sent order can be received against; a delivery refused at the dock records nothing at all. **Partially Received** and **Received** are computed from cumulative receipts per line and never stored or toggled — the stored status stays Draft, Sent, or Cancelled, and every surface reads the projection. **Close Short** is an assertion, not a status: it timestamps a Partially Received order to release the open remainder nobody expects any more, leaving the order Sent with its receipts and history intact. It needs a remainder to release, so a Sent order with no receipts and a fully Received one both refuse it, and nothing can be received against an order once it is closed short. Cancellation is allowed only while no receipts exist. An **Amendment** is how a Sent order changes: a quantity change either way, an added line, or a Part substituted on a line nothing has arrived against. Each is applied to the order's lines and appended to an insert-only log carrying who, when, and a mandatory note, then re-rendered as a further PDF **revision** — the as-sent original is never replaced, and the newest revision is the current order. Drafts take no amendments (they are edited whole) and neither does a closed-short order. A quantity may never be amended below what the line has already received. A **Credit Note** is a Supplier's credit filed into the order's own Document collection, recording which Return to Supplier movements it settles; the reference lives on the document because ledger rows are immutable, and it is per movement so an order that sent several things back and got one credit still shows the rest as **awaiting credit**. A return takes at most one credit note. Credit notes stay on the Purchase Order and are never projected onto linked Jobs the way the order PDF is. The older manually uploaded Job `purchase_order` Document remains supported as historical evidence and is distinct from the live entity.

**Quote Document** is a generated customer-facing PDF packet owned by a Quote. The newest created Quote Document is treated as the latest. Product Quote packets merge the Product's Brochure at generation time; Custom Quote packets do not include a Product Brochure.

**Brochure** is a generated customer-facing product marketing PDF produced from a Product's Brochure Config, not an uploaded file. It is generated from live config every time: streamed unsaved when viewed from the Product screen, merged into the Quote Document packet at Quote generation, and saved as a standalone Job Document at Job creation. A saved Brochure is immutable and reflects the config as it was at generation time.

**Brochure Config** is the Product-owned configuration the Brochure is generated from: subtitle, key features (an ordered list of freeform lines), and the range logo, hero, technical drawing, and secondary images. The title comes from the Product name, the assembly lists from the Product's Standard and Optional Assemblies, and the body copy from the Product description.

## Access

App Role owns authorization. Department Membership is descriptive only and must not be used to scope permissions.

- **super-admin**: everything admin can do, plus the exclusive ability to review Corrective Feedback and Internal Notes through the Feedback inbox. The only role that can see Corrective Feedback — admins cannot. Only a super-admin may grant or remove the super-admin role; admins manage every other role but cannot mint a super-admin (which would otherwise be an escalation path to Corrective Feedback).
- **admin**: full operational access; owns Bay scheduling, calendar updates, Job creation and updates, admin Bay configuration, Suppliers, and Product Unit identity and Ownership Transfers — recording a Transfer by hand is admin-only, since it asserts ownership with no commercial document behind it. Sees General Feedback on subjects it can read, like any subject reader; cannot see Corrective Feedback.
- **procurement-manager**: full Purchase Order access; inventory read/adjust and cost read/revalue; Customers, Products, Parts, Suppliers, Product Unit, and Job reads; no scheduling mutation.
- **job-viewer**: Job, Product Unit, and Bay schedule reads only.
- **sales**: Quote create/read/update, Product Unit reads (stock must be selectable on a Quote), and assistant-authored email sending.
- **stores**: Purchase Order read/receive and physical inventory read/move/adjust/count/build/close-out; cannot read inventory costs.
- **bay-operator**: no app permissions and cannot sign in unless the role gains permissions later.

Server/API checks are the security boundary. Browser access checks are UX only.

## Feedback

**Feedback** is an internal report a signed-in user submits about one subject — currently a Quote or a Job. It always has a submitter (the author) and exactly one subject, attached polymorphically the way a Document is (`subjectType ∈ {quote, job}`). Avoid Complaint, Comment, Review, Ticket, or Issue for the domain object. Visibility splits by Kind: Job General Feedback is public within the workspace (readable by anyone who can read the Job), while Quote General Feedback stays private until quote-scoped read paths exist; Corrective Feedback is readable only by super-admins. Submission requires only an authenticated session: there is no `feedback:create` permission and no subject-read gate, so any signed-in user can submit feedback about any Quote or Job. See ADR 0010.

**Feedback Kind** is exactly one of `general`, `corrective-feedback-department`, or `corrective-feedback-user`. It selects which targets the Feedback carries and who may see it.

**General Feedback** (`general`) is public only when a subject-scoped read/update surface exists. Today that is Job Feedback: anyone who can read the Job can read it with attribution (submitter, date, text, Status), and anyone who can update the Job can change its Status. Quote General Feedback remains private until the quote surface gets matching endpoints. General Feedback carries no targets and never exposes Internal Notes.

**Corrective Feedback** is Private Feedback that attributes a problem to one or more **Departments** (`corrective-feedback-department`) or one or more **Users** (`corrective-feedback-user`). Both targets are multi-select. Avoid Blame. Department targets reference the fixed Department enum; User targets reference Users. Only super-admins can read or act on it, through the Feedback inbox; the submission form marks it PRIVATE.

**Feedback Status** is the review lifecycle: `open` (initial), `resolved` (acted on), or `closed` (dismissed). A super-admin moves freely between all three, including reopening; subject writers move General Feedback just as freely. There is an open-Feedback nav indicator (a warning dot on the Feedback menu item) shown when any Feedback is `open`.

**Internal Notes** is a single mutable free-text field on a Feedback that only super-admins can read or edit.

## Assistant

**Assistant** is the in-app AI chat, enabled per user. It acts strictly as the signed-in user: its capabilities are filtered by that user's permissions, and one capability set applies to the user on every platform — web and mobile run the same Assistant, differing only in presentation. A conversation is ephemeral to the client session and is never shared between devices. Avoid Chatbot, Copilot, or Agent for the domain object.

**App Link** is an internal link the Assistant attaches to an entity it mentions — a Customer, Product, Product Unit, Quote, Job, or downloadable document. App Links use one canonical path shape regardless of platform; each client resolves an activated App Link to its own surface, and a client with no matching surface shows the entity name as plain text rather than a dead or broken link. See ADR 0013.

## Lander Localization

**Locale** is the language of public Lander presentation, identified by code. English (`en`) is the **Canonical Locale**, served at unprefixed URLs; Afrikaans (`af`) is the first translated Locale, served under a locale-prefixed URL tree. The model is locale-keyed so further Locales are additive. See ADR 0011.

**Canonical Text** is the English source text staff author on catalog entities (Products, Ranges, Variants, Assemblies). Slugs, URL parameters, and uniqueness rules derive only from Canonical Text, so URLs are identical across Locales apart from the prefix.

**Translation** is a per-field rendering of an entity's text into a Locale, stored with the entity in an envelope containing its value, Canonical Text source hash, update time, and whether staff own it manually. Every text field is translatable except Model Code. A field is **Stale** when its AI-owned envelope no longer matches Canonical Text; a manual mismatch instead **Needs Review** and never enters the AI queue. Both keep serving, while a missing field falls back to Canonical Text. Saving a manual value stamps the current source hash; reverting it to AI removes the envelope and eagerly queues regeneration. Translation state is derived from stored facts, never tracked as queue state.

**Locale Preference** is a visitor's per-browser remembered Locale. It is detected from the browser language on first visit; an explicit choice via the language dropdown overrides detection, and the Preference wins over the entered URL's Locale on arrival.

## Changelog

**Changelog** is the per-release, user-facing summary of what changed, generated at release time from the released commits, reviewed by the releaser before publishing, and grouped by **Surface** (App, Lander, Mobile). Changes with no user-visible effect are omitted rather than grouped separately. Avoid Release Notes or What's New for the domain object. A Changelog exists only for production releases.

**Changelog Entry** is one titled, described change within a Surface group of a Changelog.

**Changelog View** is a per-user high-water mark: the release time of the newest Changelog the user has acknowledged. A Changelog is **Unseen** for a user when it is newer than their Changelog View, released within the last 30 days, released after the user's account was created, and the environment is production. In the dialog, finishing or skipping advances the Changelog View past everything shown; closing does not.

## Cross-Cutting

**Audit Event** records boundary-visible changes for Customers, Jobs, Job Bays, Product Units, Products, Purchase Orders, Quotes, Suppliers, and Users. Slot create/resize/remove are not audited; Slot reorders are. Feedback is not audited.

**Dashboard** is the single signed-in landing surface. Widgets are permission-gated registry entries, and Dashboard Metrics are computed live rather than stored in reporting tables.

**Job Start Alert** is a visibility signal for an accepted Quote with no Job and no allocated Product Unit whose earliest set Preferred/Planned Delivery Date is within two calendar months from the current Johannesburg date. Allocation Quotes are excluded because a stock sale needing no work is complete without a Job.
