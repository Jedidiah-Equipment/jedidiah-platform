# JedidiahOps Context

Compact domain map for implementation and planning. Search this file first for names and invariants; read only the sections relevant to the work.

## Core Model

**Job** is one production-floor work record. Do not call it an Order, Work Order, Build, or Ticket. A **Product Job** is bound to one **Product Unit** and has a `JOB-xxxxx` code; it is either a **Build Job**, which creates that Unit, or a **Rework Job**, which fits further Assemblies to a Unit that already exists. A **Custom Job** comes from a draft, sent, or accepted Custom Quote, has no Product Unit, and uses its Quote's Work Title as its display name. Every Job has a Product Unit, a Quote, or both: a Custom Job always has a Quote, a **Stock Build** has only a Unit, and build-to-order and Rework Jobs have both. A Job also carries a freeform **Description**. A Job displays the Owner of its Product Unit, or its Quote's Customer when it has no Unit; a Job on an unowned Unit reads as **Stock**. A **Cancelled Job** (its `cancelledAt` is set) is produced two ways. **Job Cancellation** is the direct one: an administrator abandons the Job itself from the Job sheet (`job:cancel`), giving a mandatory **Cancellation Reason**, and its Quote is left standing — the cascade runs one way only. It is refused on a Job that is already cancelled and on one carrying a Job Completion, which is where the two paths part: cancelling a Quote says the deal died and reaches even a completed Job, while cancelling a Job says the work will never happen, and a completion date has already said otherwise. The other way is that Quote cascade, which records its reason on the Quote and leaves the Job's own reason null. Either way a Cancelled Job is hidden from every live list, search, and picker, and all Job mutations reject it, with one carve-out: its remaining Slots may still be removed manually. Cancelling frees the Job's Quote to source a replacement while the Quote stays Locked to the accepted terms it has already supplied. A replacement Build Job reuses the cancelled Job's Product Unit and ownership history while the Quote's Customer still owns that Unit; if the Unit was removed or its ownership moved elsewhere, the replacement mints a new one. Its **retained** `done` and `active` Slots stay on the Board and Calendar as read-only history (styled Cancelled); the Job sheet opens read-only. Cancellation is terminal — there is no un-cancel.

**Job Completion** is the Job's stored `completedOn`: the plant business date it finished, or `null` while open. It **latches** — once set, nothing rewrites or clears it automatically, so a resized upstream Slot can never make a recorded completion date drift. A human sets or clears it on the Job sheet (`job:update`, no future dates); the nightly **completion sweep** otherwise stamps every open, non-cancelled Job whose Work Slots are all `done` with that Job's latest `lastWorkDay`. The sweep is strictly additive (`NULL` → date only), so backfilling history is just its first run, and it audits each write with a null (system) actor — the audit log is the only record of a date's origin. A Job with no Work Slot is never swept; a Cancelled Job is abandoned, not completed. Keep this distinct from **schedule completeness** (every Work Slot `done` against plant today), which stays derived, reacts to Slot changes instantly, and alone owns Active Board windowing and the Book Slot picker's Active filter. The Job List and the inventory close-out queue read `completedOn`, as does that picker's Unscheduled filter — a Job with no Work Slot has no completeness to derive from, so the stamp is the only thing that ever retires it from that list.

**Completed Job Sales Export** is the Job List's CSV of finished work: one row per completed, live Job carrying its Job, Quote and Invoice Numbers, its Customer, its Product Serial Number and Product, the completion date, and cost against retail both ex- and VAT-inclusive. Cost is the Job's drawn material at its stamped movement cost — never re-priced, and blank rather than zero when the Job still holds material with no cost established, the same rule Job Material Variance follows; retail is its Quote Pricing subtotal and total, blank on a Stock Build. The two are not comparable to the cent, because cost carries no labour, so the export reports them side by side and computes no margin. It takes the Job List's own filters and is never paginated, completion is its subject rather than a filter it accepts, and the whole report demands `inventory_cost:read`, `job:read` and `quote:read` together — one row crosses all three, so holding one of them entitles a reader to none of the rest.

**Product Unit** is one physical machine. It owns the **Product Serial Number**, the Product it was built as, and an optional **VIN Number**. It is created with its Build Job and never on its own; a Custom Job produces none. **Unit Removal** is the one way out: an administrator deletes a machine that was never built — every Job on it Cancelled and none of them carrying a Job Completion, and no Quote that still stands naming it. A latched Job Completion is the record that metal was cut, so a Job completed before its Quote was cancelled keeps the machine. One rule governs what survives: cancelled records stand and lose only their link to the machine, so a cancelled Job keeps its Quote, a cancelled Quote keeps everything but the serial it named, and a cancelled **Stock Build** ends up holding neither Unit nor Quote — allowed, because it still records an intention that was abandoned. The Unit's ownership history goes with it. An Owner does not refuse removal: a build-to-order Unit is owned from the moment it is minted, so a phantom from a sale that died is owned by definition, and only a person can say the machine never existed. Removal is never a side effect but always an explicit choice, offered at the moment of cancelling a Quote or a Stock Build — ticked by default only when no Slot has started and no stock has been drawn against the Job — and available afterwards from the Unit itself. The Serial is not reclaimed: the per-Product sequence never rewinds, so a gap in the serials records a build that was started and abandoned. Its **As-Built Spec** is the union of its Jobs' Build Specs, counting only live Jobs — a Cancelled Job's CFO is a plan, not a record of what was fitted. A Unit is **In Build** until its Build Job has a Job Completion and **On Hand** from then on, both derived from that Job and never stored; a Cancelled Job is skipped, so a rebuilt Unit reads from the build that actually ran. A Unit that is On Hand and owned by a Customer reads as **Complete**: a label over the build state and the Owner together, applied at the surface rather than a third state of the machine, so the Units list offers In Build, On Hand, and Complete as filters and its On Hand narrows to the ones we still hold. Avoid Machine, Item, Asset, or Stock Item.

**Unit Stock Export** is the Units list's CSV of the machines that exist: one row per On Hand Product Unit carrying its Product Serial Number and Product, its Build Job's Job Number and Job Completion, the Quote and Invoice Numbers and Customer of the sale that placed it, and cost against Product retail both ex- and VAT-inclusive. Cost is what the Unit's live Jobs drew in material at stamped movement cost, summed across the build and every rework — never re-priced, and blank rather than zero when any of those Jobs holds material with no cost established, the same rule the Completed Job Sales Export follows. An open Rework Job counts, so an On Hand Unit under further work reports a cost that is still moving. Retail is the **Product's base price as the catalog holds it today**, not what the machine fetched, so the two are neither comparable to the cent nor a margin the export computes. The sale's facts come off the Transfer that decides the Unit's Owner, which is the build's Quote for a machine built to order and the Allocation Quote for one sold out of stock, and is blank on Stock and on a Transfer recorded by hand. It takes the Units list's own filters and is never paginated; being On Hand is its subject rather than a filter it accepts, so **On Hand** narrows it to the machines we still hold and **Complete** to the ones already sold, while **In Build** contradicts the subject and is dropped — a filter that cannot narrow the report must not empty it instead, the same rule under which the Job export ignores Include Completed. The whole report demands `inventory_cost:read`, `product:read`, `product_unit:read` and `quote:read` together — one row crosses all four, so holding one of them entitles a reader to none of the rest.

**Unit Ownership** is an append-only log of **Ownership Transfers** on one Product Unit: from Customer (null means we hold it), to Customer, the date it happened, an optional sourcing Quote, and the actor. The current **Owner** is the newest Transfer's destination and is never a column; a Unit with no Transfers is **Stock**, meaning we hold it. Owner is the Customer our records attribute the machine to — not legal title, payment, or delivery — so a build-to-order Unit is owned by its buyer while it is still in a Bay. A Transfer is written when a build-to-order Unit is created, when an Allocation Quote is accepted, and by hand for a resale between Customers or a return to Stock. Cancelling a Quote that moved ownership writes a reversing Transfer rather than erasing the round trip.

**Build Spec** is a Job's own selection of Optional Assemblies and the only source a CFO is snapshotted from. A Quote's selected Assemblies *seed* it at Job creation the way the Work Item Rate Card seeds a rate; a Stock Build enters it directly. A Rework Job's Build Spec is only the Assemblies being added, so its CFO covers that work alone.

**Stock Build** is a Job with a Build Spec and no Quote. It builds a Product Unit we hold, with no Customer and no commercial record until the Unit sells. There is no Customer record standing in for the company itself.

**Quote** is a sales offer for one **Customer**, owned by one salesperson — a `sales`, `admin`, or `super-admin` user, which is narrower than the roles that may raise a Quote: procurement holds `quote:create` but is never named as the salesperson on one. A **Product Quote** references one Product, can source at most one live Job once accepted, and becomes a **Locked Quote** once it has ever sourced a Job: commercial facts stay frozen after Job Cancellation frees it to source a replacement, while post-sale logistics and notes can still change where the UI allows. An **Allocation Quote** is a Product Quote that also references an existing Product Unit, selling a machine we already hold instead of one to be built: its selected Optional Assemblies are seeded from the Unit's As-Built Spec and all charge as if built to order, it locks on acceptance rather than on sourcing a Job, and the only live Job it can source is a Rework Job. Accepting one transfers ownership and is refused unless the Unit is Stock at that moment, so any number of draft or sent Quotes may compete for one Unit but only one can close. **Product Source** is what the surfaces call that distinction: an Allocation Quote reads **From Stock** and every other Product Quote reads **From Order**. It is derived from whether the Quote names a Unit, never stored, and a Custom Quote has none, so its Product Source reads as nothing at all. Because that lock lands before the sale is settled, an accepted Allocation Quote is the one Locked Quote whose **discount** can still move, under the ordinary `quote:update`; every other commercial fact on it stays frozen, and a cancelled one moves nothing. **Invoice Number** is the Quote's record of the sale's invoice; it stays editable while locked, because a stock sale needing no work never sources a Job. A **Custom Quote** has no Product and no base price (it is forced to zero); it is priced entirely from a Work Title and its Work Items. It can source at most one live Custom Job while draft, sent, or accepted, and locks commercial facts once accepted except that its Work Items remain editable. A Quote can be **Cancelled** into a terminal, irreversible status that also locks it. **Quote Cancellation** is the single path, reached from the status field on an unlocked Quote and from a dedicated Cancel Quote action on a Locked one, whose status field is closed; a status change can never itself be what cancels, because cancelling settles the Job and the machine too. The permission follows the lock: an unlocked Quote needs only `quote:update`, since undoing paperwork nobody has acted on is the salesperson's own, while a Locked one needs `quote:cancel`. Cancelling **always** returns a machine this Quote sold to Stock as a reversing Transfer, and then offers two choices. **Cancel the live Job** is ticked by default and removes its future (`scheduled`) Slots in one transaction, reaching even a completed Job — unlike direct Job Cancellation, which refuses one; declining it leaves the build live and its machine with the Customer. **Remove the Unit** that Job was building is offered only alongside that cancellation and only for a machine the Job minted, ticked by default only when nothing has started. Historical Cancelled Jobs stay unchanged. The remedy for a mistaken Quote cancellation is a new Quote.

**Customer** means the company buying or receiving the product. A Customer may be Removed only while no Quote,
Ownership Transfer, or other entity references it; removal permanently deletes the directory record. Avoid Client or
Buyer in code and docs.

**Product Range** is an admin-managed catalog grouping intended for Product marketing and selection. Every Product belongs to exactly one Range. A Range carries a name and an optional presentation image for customer-facing documents. Avoid Category, Line, or Family. Range management (create, update, and the management list) is admin-only; Product forms load Range options through Product access.

**Product Range Variant** is an admin-managed, ordered sub-grouping within one Range, carrying only a name. A Product optionally belongs to exactly one Variant, which must be a Variant of its Range; changing a Product's Range clears its Variant. A Range may have no Variants. Variant names are unique within their Range, not globally. A Variant cannot be removed while any Product references it. Variants exist for marketing and selection refinement. Avoid Model, Trim, Sub-range, or Option (Option already means Optional Assembly selection on Quotes). Variant management lives with Range management and is admin-only; Product forms load Variant options through Product access.

**Supplier** is currently a standalone procurement directory record. It cannot be removed while it has a draft or approved Purchase Order, neither of which has reached the Supplier yet and would be stranded; sent and cancelled orders keep their Supplier as history. **Part** is the reusable stockable catalog item, bought or built. A Part's Unit of Measure is its counting unit everywhere. A linear Part uses `mm` and must have a positive **Standard Purchase Length** in millimetres; `mm` marks the class its lengths are measured in, never what its quantities count — linear stock is always counted in whole pieces, and metres are display formatting of `mm` rather than a second unit. **Storage Location** is findability-only, never a stock-ledger dimension. A Part's Unit of Measure freezes once the Part has a Stock Movement, since changing it would silently rewrite what that ledger fact counted. Its Supplier freezes once the Part appears on a Purchase Order that was not cancelled, since changing it would silently rewrite what that order asked for. A Part has **either a Supplier or a BOM — never both, never neither**: a **Built Part** carries a Bill of Materials instead of a Supplier and is made in-house, so it can never sit on a Purchase Order line. It is never linear either: a Build posts one row at consumed value ÷ units built and names no length bucket, so linear stock is bought and cut rather than made. Everything else about it is unchanged — it is stocked, checked out, costed, labelled, and specced into a CFO exactly like a bought Part. Its BOM may be empty, which is the trivial build of a Part whose components are all raw material. **Assembly** is a Product-owned grouping of Parts, either Standard or Optional; an Assembly's kind is immutable after creation. Optional Assemblies carry an upgrade-delta price, may replace whole Standard Assemblies, and are selected on Product Quotes. An Assembly is **Publicly Visible** only when it may appear on the Lander and Brochure; this defaults off for new Assemblies and does not affect internal catalog, Quote, or production use.

**Parts Bulk CSV** is the one file the Parts catalog goes out on and comes back in on: the Bulk Parts Export writes it and the Bulk Parts Import reads it, over the same columns in the same order, so a file taken out, edited, and put back lines up. It carries only what identifies a Part in the catalog — its Code, Drawing Code, Description, Supplier and Supplier Code, Finish, Category, Name, Unit of Measure, whether it is a Built Part, and its Standard Purchase Length. Storage Location, minimum stock and the perpetual/periodic choice are not the CSV's, so an import leaves them as it found them. **Part Code is the match key, and it alone**: a row whose Code is already in the catalog updates that Part, a row whose Code is new creates one, and a row that changes nothing counts as neither. Code does not override identity, though — a Part is its Code plus who supplies it, so a row whose Code exists under a different Supplier is refused as a conflict rather than re-pointed, and the rest of the file still imports. The export takes `part:read`, the same rows the Parts list already hands a reader; the import takes `part:update`. Either may be scoped to one Supplier.

**Product Material List** is a Product's periodic-stock raw materials and quantities consumed per Product Unit. It sits beside, not inside, Assemblies: fabricated Parts in an Assembly carry their raw material through this Product-level list. **Product Labor Hours** is the Product-level standard hours per production Department, also per Unit.

**Product Cost Estimate** is the live floor of Product Material List cost, effective Assembly Part cost, and Product Labor Hours at the shared Department rates. Inventory terms use current moving-average costs. A fabricated Part with no moving average contributes zero because its material belongs to the Product Material List; a bought Part with no moving average is missing data. Missing material, labor, or bought-Part costs make the result an explicitly incomplete `≥` floor, never a confident partial. An Optional Assembly's cost is always labelled partial because Product-level material and labor cannot be attributed to it.

**Job Estimate Snapshot** is the Product Cost Estimate, including its missing-input flags, frozen on a Product Job inside Job creation. A Build Job freezes the full Product estimate. A Rework Job freezes only the Optional Assembly Parts in its Rework CFO; Product-level material and labor stay explicitly unattributed rather than being charged to the Rework. A Custom Job has no snapshot. The Job's estimate-vs-actual comparison compares the snapshot's Assembly Part floor with costs stamped on its Checkouts; frozen material and labor remain context because neither has a comparable Checkout total. **Raw Material Drift** compares physical depletion between two raw-material stocktake sessions with material lines frozen on the snapshots of Product Jobs completed in that window. Rework snapshots contribute no Product-level material. Its expected figure is a floor because Jobs without a completion date or estimate snapshot do not count.

A **Part Label** is an on-demand printable identity for exactly one Part: Code 128 encodes only the Part code, while readable text repeats its code, name, and Storage Location. Labels are never stored or tracked, are never scoped by Supplier, and linear labels stay per Part rather than per length bucket.

## Stock

A **Stock Movement** is one append-only, actor-attributed ledger fact that changes a Part's stock or cost; it is never edited or deleted. Stock on hand is the sum of movement deltas per Part, bucketed by length for linear Parts. A **Checkout** draws a Part from stock against a Job at the current moving average; a **Return to Store** reverses it at the cost still drawn by that Job, and stays available after the Job is cancelled so recovered stock is not stranded off-ledger. A **Receipt** is the arrival of stock against one open Purchase Order line — it attaches to that line, defaults its cost from the line's price and its length from the Part's standard purchase length, and is the ledger write receiving *is*, never a paper step posted later. A **Return to Supplier** sends received stock back off the line it arrived on, at that line's stamped receipt cost and under a reason (`wrong-item`, `defective`, `order-error`) — value-neutral for the Part, since the quantity leaving is worth what it was worth arriving. It needs a Receipt behind it — every surface offers it only on a line with received quantity — though the ledger keys nothing on that, so a return past what the line took in posts with a warning like any other quantity disagreement. It stays available after an order is closed short, and is the one consumption-shaped movement a periodic Part accepts, because it reverses an arrival rather than recording use. Its reason decides whether the line re-opens: `wrong-item` and `defective` say a replacement is owed, so the return nets off what the line has received and the order waits on it again — On Order included — while `order-error` says nothing is coming in its place and leaves the line as received. Both Job movements are valid off-CFO — a draw against a Part the Job's CFO never planned is legitimate and warns about nothing, because "past the CFO" means nothing where there is no CFO; the Job Material Variance report is where unplanned material is answered for. Every movement warns rather than blocks when quantities disagree: a draw past a CFO that planned the Part, a draw taking a length bucket negative, a return past what the Job still holds, a receipt past what the line ordered, and a return to the Supplier past what the line received are all warnings, judged once and shown both before the post and on it. **Stock Movement Facts** are what "judged once" rests on: the server derives what a movement is judged against and carries it on the reads a surface previews from, so a preview adds the keyed quantity to served facts rather than re-deriving a threshold of its own. A return is judged against the length bucket it names, never the Part's total. The post re-judges under its lock and stays authoritative, so a surface reports only what the post found on top of what was already confirmed. A movement carries whichever reference explains it — the order it arrived on, the Job it was drawn to, or the stocktake walk that counted it — and a Part's history names that reference so any row can be traced back to what caused it. A **Build** is the production event that turns a Built Part's components into finished units: one transaction, value-preserving, consuming each component at its stamped moving average and producing a single row at consumed value ÷ units built — or at no cost at all when nothing consumed carried one. Consumption prefills at BOM × N and is edited to what actually left the rack; deviating from the BOM warns, and a short rack goes negative, because the build already happened. BOMs nest but **builds never recurse**: components come from stock, so inner batches are built to the rack before outer builds consume them, and raw material posts no consumption at all. **Commitment** is derived per Job and Part as `max(0, CFO quantity − drawn quantity)` until inventory close-out; **Free Stock** is stock on hand minus the sum of open commitments. **Close-out** is the inventory-local assertion that a Job's stock life has ended: recorded once against a completed, live Job, it releases that Job's remaining commitment permanently — later movements change what is drawn but never re-open the released demand. It is not a Job status, and reopening is not a concept; cancellation releases commitment its own way and is never closed out. The **close-out queue** is every completed Job still holding drawn stock or open commitment, ordered oldest first — a Job leaves it by being closed out, or by having nothing outstanding left to close, and the days it has waited are the stale-commitment signal. **Job Material Variance** is one Job's plan against what it actually took: CFO quantity per Part summed across its assemblies, drawn quantity net of returns, and what those draws cost at the price they were stamped with — never re-priced at today's average, and carrying no planned cost at all, because the CFO froze quantities and not money. Parts a Job drew that its CFO never planned report as rows of their own, and what they cost is called out beside the Job total that contains it, since that unplanned material is what the report is read for. Length buckets are summed away — a Job is over or under on a Part — and the report reads the same on a live Job as on a completed or closed-out one.

**On Order** is the sum of `ordered − received` over the open lines of Sent Purchase Orders that have not been closed short — the cover the shelf does not hold yet. It is always shown *beside* Free Stock and never folded into it. **Minimum Stock** is an optional per-Part reorder level, edited on the Part like any other attribute. The **buy list** is every Part carrying at least one of three tagged reasons — Free Stock negative, stock on hand below Minimum Stock, or stock on hand at or below zero — ranked by the earliest unfinished Work Slot date among the Jobs whose commitment drives it, with Parts no scheduled Job is waiting on ranked last. Its **suggested buy** is the largest of those shortfalls less On Order, floored at zero. Nothing on the buy list ever raises an order by itself: a ticked selection splits into one Draft Purchase Order per Supplier, with each line's editable price defaulted from the Part's current moving average where one exists and left unpriced where none does; a Built Part can never be ticked because it has no Supplier to buy it from. A **Late Purchase Order** is a Sent order past its expected delivery date, not closed short, with lines still owed. **Returns awaiting credit** is every Return to Supplier movement no credit note references — money the plant is still owed, keyed per movement so a partly credited order keeps showing the rest.

A **Stocktake Session** is one counting walk: opened with a **Scope**, counted Part by Part, and closed once. The two scopes are the plant's two standing rhythms — raw material weekly, stores monthly — and a Part belongs to the scope its Stock Tracking Mode implies at the moment it is counted, never to a stored membership list. At most one session per scope is open at a time; the open one is resumed rather than replaced. A **Stock Count** posts `stock-count` delta adjustments stamped with the session, computed against stock on hand at the moment of the count so a mid-session Receipt is never counted away, and never an overwrite. A count covers the whole Part: every length bucket holding stock must be named, an empty one by counting zero, and a count leaving one unnamed is refused rather than inferred empty — an inferred zero is indistinguishable from stock that arrived mid-count, and writing that off is the one mistake a stocktake must not make. A count that agreed still posts a zero-delta row, because that row is the only record that the Part was counted at all. Counting the same Part twice in a session is legal and reports as two rows, each attributed to its own counter; the second corrects against what the first left behind rather than doubling it. Ad-hoc counts outside any session stay legal, and those still carry the mandatory note every other adjustment does — a session-stamped count is the one adjustment excused from it, because the session it names says more about why the quantity moved than free text would. Entry is **blind** — the tablet hides recorded stock until a figure is keyed, then shows expected beside counted for a one-tap recount before anything is posted. There is no approval gate; the control is the **session variance report** (every counted Part with its delta, priced for cost readers, and the **skip list** of in-scope Parts the walk never reached — both derived, never stored). A scope is **overdue** when no closed session covers it within its cadence plus grace (raw material a week plus two working days, stores a month plus five), and a scope never counted is overdue outright.

**Stock Tracking Mode** is per Part: perpetual records every stock movement, while periodic records only its opening balance, its receipts, and its stocktake counts — a periodic Part rejects consumption movements and every other adjustment reason outright, because between counts its stock on hand is deliberately stale-high and only a count can correct it. Receipts are deliberately *not* among what it rejects: bought-in raw material still arrives on a Purchase Order, and the receipt is the baseline the next count corrects.

**Average Utilization %** is an optional Part field, valid only on discrete periodic Parts. Setting it declares that the Part is a plate-like item consumed fractionally by Jobs: once frozen Product Job material-line demand reaches that percentage of a plate's total area, the remainder is skeleton or scrap and the physical plate is gone. It is freely editable because it shapes estimates rather than ledger facts. **Estimated Stock on Hand** is the derived, never-stored expectation of what the next count will find for such a Part: whole untouched plates plus the remaining fraction of the one open plate. Demand accrues when the Job is created, excludes cancelled Jobs, starts at the Part's first ledger movement, and carries the open plate across Stock Counts; a count re-anchors the whole-plate quantity without resetting that partial. One open plate per Part is assumed. The buy list uses Estimated Stock on Hand's whole plates for that Part's stock on hand and Free Stock signals; the ledger, blind stocktake entry, and Stock Movement rules remain unchanged.

**Part Stock Actions** are the derived per-Part verdicts — adjust, build, checkout, count, purchase, receive, return to store, return to supplier, and revalue — computed from the Part's own stored facts: whether it is a Built Part, its Stock Tracking Mode, and its unit class. They are never stored, and one derivation answers both the surfaces and the server's own write gates, so a control is never offered for a write the post would refuse. A blocked action carries the reason it is blocked, which is the fact itself rather than a message. They judge the *Part*: a check that judges an input instead — an adjustment reason, a unit cost, a bucket a count left unnamed — stays with the write that reads that input, and a Part's membership of a Stocktake Session's scope stays with the session. Purchase Order Actions judge the order, so a surface offers a receive when the Part allows it and the order allows it. Permissions are a separate seam, so a surface renders a control when the role allows it and the Part allows it. Adjust and count are always allowed, because no Part refuses either outright: a periodic Part still takes an opening balance and a stock count, and every Part is countable in the scope its Stock Tracking Mode implies. What each still refuses — the other adjustment reasons, a session of the wrong scope — is judged by the write and the session, not by the Part. They are in the shape so a surface reads one contract.

A Part's moving average cost is derived from ordered cost-bearing movements, is per millimetre for linear Parts, and remains absent — “no cost yet,” never zero — until a cost is established. Internally fabricated Parts are the deliberate exception: they carry zero material cost because their raw material is charged separately. Costs are gated: a caller without inventory cost access reads every cost field as null, on every surface that carries one.

**Work Item** is a Custom Quote charge describing one unit of custom work. Every Work Item carries an optional description, a labour duration in hours (fractional, zero allowed), the ex-VAT **hourly rate** it was priced at, and zero or more Work Item Parts. It is named one of two ways: a **departmental Work Item** takes its name from its Department and stores none, while the department-less **Other** Work Item is the only shape that carries its own name. Its total is hours × its own rate plus the sum of its Parts, rounded to cents; it contributes to the discountable ex-VAT subtotal. Labour-only, parts-only, and zero-cost Work Items are all legal, as is a Custom Quote with no Work Items. Work Items are an explicit commercial-fact exception to Quote locking and remain editable after acceptance and after a Custom Job starts. The Custom Job displays its Quote's Work Items by name only; the customer-facing Quote Document prints each Work Item as name + optional description, with a positive-duration Labour row (hours + hourly rate + subtotal) followed by its Parts (quantity + name + unit price + subtotal). Zero-hour Work Items omit the Labour row. A Work Item heading prints only what those rows leave unaccounted for, so the Subtotal column sums: nothing at all on a Work Item its rows cover in full, and an **Other** line's flat amount — never labour, so never a row of its own — where it also carries Parts. A Work Item with no rows beneath it prints its own total, down to the zero of a no-charge inclusion. The Quote editor aside and the mobile summary drawer show every Work Item total regardless.

**Work Item Rate Card** is the default ex-VAT hourly rate per Department, held in `@pkg/domain` (`WORK_ITEM_DEPARTMENT_RATES`). It only *seeds* a Work Item: each row snapshots the rate it was priced at, so editing the card never reprices an existing Quote, and one Quote may mix Departments at different rates. A Department becomes quotable by gaining a rate on the card — the database check already permits the full Department enum, so no migration is involved. Unrated Departments seed at zero. There is no Quote-level hourly rate.

**Work Item Part** is a freeform row on one Work Item — required name, positive whole-number quantity, non-negative unit price. It is *not* a catalog Part and never references one; the resemblance is sales-facing only.

**Quote Pricing** is the computed breakdown — ex-VAT subtotal, VAT amount/rate, VAT-inclusive customer total, discount amount, Product Quote live selected Optional Assemblies, and Work Items (Custom Quotes) — projected from a Quote's stored pricing facts. Product Quotes latch base price/currency from Product at creation; Custom Quotes hold a zero base price, price entirely from Work Items, and use `ZAR`. A selection is excluded when stale. On persisted Product Quotes a `null` reference is the complete stale set (Assembly kind is immutable and deletion is `on delete set null`); while editing, in-flight selections are additionally resolved against the freshly loaded catalog. Live selections list in catalog display order on every surface. Deposit (a payment term) is not an input. Per-Quote price surfaces use the VAT-inclusive total; reporting aggregates explicitly labelled ex-VAT use the subtotal.

**CFO** means Customer Fabrication Order: the Job's frozen bill of materials, snapshotted from the Job's Build Spec at Job creation.

## Manufacturing

The visual pipeline is fixed: Fabrication -> Procurement -> Supply -> Paint -> Assembly -> Workshop. A **Department** groups **Bays**, but is not an authorization boundary. **Workshop** is distinct from Assembly, and the two retain distinct names on Quotes.

**Bay** is a durable physical workspace. Avoid Station, Workstation, Cell, or Machine. Bays are created, renamed, disabled, and re-enabled by admins. Disabled Bays reject new bookings but keep existing schedule history. Deleting is only for a Bay added in error: a Bay any Slot, Product Bay, or Operator Assignment references — open or closed — is refused and disabled instead, while its Bay Calendar Exceptions go with it.

**Bay Operator** is a permissionless shop-floor user role. An **Operator Assignment** attaches one Bay Operator to one Bay until unassigned; the current operator is the open assignment interval, not a column on the Bay.

**Product Bay** is a Product's default Bay plus working-day duration. It seeds Job scheduling, but it is not required and does not derive from quote lead time.

**Department Timing** is a Job's observed `startedAt`/`completedAt` for one work Department (`fabrication`, `paint`, `assembly`, `workshop` — the same set Product Labor Hours restricts to), stamped by hand (`job:update`), never derived from the schedule, and never read by the Board or the scheduler — an observation log, not the removed per-job stage rows. The done-stamp is the department's manager stopping that work, distinct from Job Completion, which the factory manager records later. Stamps and crew stay editable while the Job is live and lock when `completedOn` latches. Only Fabrication has UI.

**Department Crew** is the set of Bay Operators recorded on a done-stamp as having crewed that Department's work; a Fabrication crew member is a **Fabricator**. At least one per done-stamp, role-validated like Operator Assignment.

**Product Build Metrics** are the elapsed working days between a Department's stamps across a Product's Build Jobs, with the per-Fabricator ranking behind `job_metrics:read`. Live-computed, never stored — the same rule Dashboard Metrics follow — and Build Jobs only.

## Scheduling

**Bay Queue** is the ordered Slot sequence for one Bay from its `scheduleOrigin`. All queue mutations serialize on the Bay row. Avoid using Schedule for this per-Bay sequence.

**Slot** is a whole-day planning block in a Bay Queue. Avoid Booking, Reservation, Appointment, or Block. A **Work Slot** references one Job. An **Idle Slot** reserves working time without a Job and is distinct from an Off-Day.

Slot dates are derived, never stored on Slots. Projection walks a Bay Queue from the Bay's plant business-date origin, consuming `durationDays` as working days and skipping Off-Days. Derived dates are `yyyy-MM-dd` plant business dates and must read the same in every viewer timezone. Africa/Johannesburg is used only at the server boundary to derive plant "today" and new Bay origins.

A Slot's span is half-open `[startDate, endDate)` and tiles its Bay Queue without gaps, so a span can open on an Off-Day. Projection therefore also emits **`firstWorkDay`** and **`lastWorkDay`** — the inclusive working days the Slot actually covers, snapped onto the Working Calendar. Geometry (Gantt bars, segments, day breakdowns) reads the raw span; every human-readable date label reads `firstWorkDay`/`lastWorkDay`, never raw span arithmetic.

**Working Calendar** is explicit dated facts. Unmarked dates are working days. **Off-Days** are explicit non-working dates. **Bay Calendar Exceptions** override the org calendar for one Bay: Overtime opens an otherwise-off day; Bay Closure closes an otherwise-working day. Off-Days are stepped over by projection and are not Idle Slots.

**Job Days-Left** is a Job-level horizon, distinct from a single Slot's remaining days: the maximum, over the Job's unfinished Work Slots, of the working days from plant "today" through that Slot's last work day. It answers when the Job is fully off the floor, paced by its last Bay, and counts the working days in any idle gap before a not-yet-started Slot.

**Insert at Date** is a placement hint, not a stored or pinned start. Under the Bay lock, the chosen date resolves against live projection: inside a Slot splits it, exactly at a Slot start inserts before it, and past the next available day appends. Deliberate deferral beyond the queue end is represented with Idle Slots.

**Board** is the full cross-Bay projected state: every Bay Queue projected against its Working Calendar, with each Slot's state (done, active, or scheduled against plant "today") and cross-Bay Job completion resolved. It is built in one pass from stored facts and never stored itself; every schedule read is a slice of the Board. Avoid Shop or Schedule for this whole.

**Active Board** is the default window over the Board, as read through `jobs.listBays`: every Slot of a Job that still has an unfinished Work Slot, unioned with Slots ending on or after a `from` floor (default plant "today"). Fully-complete Jobs — every Slot's projected `endDate` at or before today — drop off, bounding the shared read to work-in-progress plus forward bookings. The server projects the full queue and computes the cross-Bay Job-completion map before trimming; only the `slots` array is windowed, never `nextAvailableDate` or other queue-end scalars. The Gantt scrolls into history by lowering its own `from` (clamped to ~12 months back) on the same query. Projection-dependent previews (insert-seeds placement and ghost bars) resolve server-side via `jobs.previewSchedule`; the Working Calendar stays client-side. See ADR 0009.

## Commercial Documents

**Document** is a stored file an owner accumulates in a collection — it carries its own filename, owner-type-specific metadata, and identity. Its stored content and metadata are immutable, although an owner-specific policy may allow its collection row to be deleted. It is distinct from a single named file held directly as a field of an entity (such as a Product's hero or logo image), which is not a Document. Reserve Document for the collection member; avoid Attachment, Media, or Asset. File is not a domain object: it names the shared stored-file primitive (content-type agnostic) that both a Document and an entity's file fields are built on. Documents use private object storage through the API; deleting the row does not delete the stored object. Metadata is immutable and owner-type specific.

**Thumbnail** is separate: a small inline display image for quick recognition.

**Job Document Snapshot** copies the Product's current uploaded Documents onto a Product Job at Job creation, pointing to the same immutable stored files and freezing metadata. The Brochure is not copied but generated fresh and saved as its own Job Document. Custom Jobs start with an empty Documents collection and do not generate a Brochure. Product display names may still read live. Snapshot Documents and the saved Brochure cannot be deleted.

**Purchase Order** is the live procurement order placed with exactly one Supplier. It starts as a Draft whose expected delivery date, Part lines, and optional links to one or more Jobs are one editable aggregate saved together; an order with no linked Jobs is restock. A line records a quantity in the Part's purchasing unit and its agreed unit price, and every line's Part must belong to the order's Supplier. A line's price is a stored fact, so only the API's cost gate can hide it — which also means editing a draft needs inventory cost access, not just Purchase Order rights. **Approval** sits between Draft and Sent: one administrator sign-off, gated by `purchase_order:approve` and timestamped on the order, that an order must carry before it can be marked Sent. There is no four-eyes rule and no submit-for-approval queue — any holder approves any Draft, including their own, and who drafted and who approved are separately visible in the audit trail. Approving freezes editing exactly as sending does, so the one way back is **Revert to Draft**, an audited un-approval gated by that same permission which clears the timestamp and reopens the aggregate. The actor lives in the audit trail rather than a column, as it does for sending. Amending a Sent order never disturbs its approval: the sign-off covers the Draft-to-Sent transition, and post-Sent change is the amendment log's jurisdiction. Marking it Sent is a human assertion that freezes editing and saves one immutable, Purchase-Order-owned PDF containing the order number, linked Job codes, Part quantities, and the last modifier's name and date — never prices. That same Document is projected into every linked Job's Documents tab without copying its bytes or ownership, where the existing cost-reader gate still controls its visibility. Only a sent order can be received against; a delivery refused at the dock records nothing at all. **Partially Received** and **Received** are computed from cumulative receipts per line and never stored or toggled — the stored status stays Draft, Approved, Sent, or Cancelled, and every surface reads the projection. Sent is deliberately absent from that projection: an order that has gone out with nothing received against it reads **Approved**, and whether it has actually gone to the Supplier is carried by the Sent tick beside the status on the order list. Sent implies Approved for every order including history, so nothing downstream of sending special-cases an order that predates approval. **Close Short** is an assertion, not a status: it timestamps a Sent order to release the open remainder nobody expects any more, leaving the order Sent with its receipts and history intact. It needs ledger history behind it to close short of — anything at all having moved against the order, receipts and returns alike — and a remainder to release, so an untouched order and a fully Received one both refuse it, and nothing can be received against an order once it is closed short. Cancellation reads that same history fact from the other side and is allowed only while the order has none — an Approved order joins Draft and Sent as cancellable under that same guard, discarding a sign-off the audit trail keeps, so no order can ever refuse both for want of history: history is the ledger rows and never the netted quantities, and only the remainder is netted. An order whose receipts have all gone back as replacement-owed returns is owed everything again and reads Approved, but its rows are real: it is closed short, never cancelled. An **Amendment** is how a Sent order changes: a quantity change either way, an added line, a Part substituted on a line nothing has arrived against, or a changed expected delivery date. Each is applied to the order and appended to an insert-only log carrying who, when, and a mandatory note, then re-rendered as a further PDF **revision** — the as-sent original is never replaced, and the newest revision is the current order. Drafts take no amendments (they are edited whole) and neither does a closed-short order. A quantity may never be amended below what the line has already received. **Purchase Order Actions** are the derived per-order verdicts — edit, approve, revert to draft, send, receive, amend, return to supplier, cancel, close short, and file documents — computed from the stored status, the close-short assertion, and the same ledger history and netted remainder the termination rules read. They are never stored, and one derivation answers both the surfaces and the server's own write gates, so a control is never offered for a write the post would refuse. A blocked action carries the reason it is blocked, which is the state itself rather than a message. They judge the *order*: a check that judges an input instead — an unpriced line, a quantity below what a line has received, substituting a Part that has taken delivery — stays with the write that reads that input. Permissions are a separate seam, so a surface renders a control when the role allows it and the order allows it. A **Supplier Invoice** is the Supplier's bill for what arrived, filed into the same collection and read once by AI into a stored **Invoice Extraction** — lines, invoice number and date, and any Part or Job codes the Supplier echoed. The **cross-check** between that extraction and the order's lines is never stored: it is recomputed against the order as it stands on every read, so an amendment agreeing a price answers its flag by itself. A line is matched by Part code, then Supplier code, then description similarity, and each disagreement — price, quantity, a billed line no order line matches, an order line the invoice did not bill — is a **flag** a cost reader judges. Flags are advisory: applying a price disagreement is the human confirmation that posts a Revaluation moving the Part's average by the value its Receipts got wrong, spread over stock still on hand, and it is refused once that stock has been drawn; dismissing one sets it aside permanently. One flag takes one answer, an unreadable invoice is a supported outcome that blocks nothing, and **PO vs invoiced** is every priced disagreement plant-wide with what was done about it. A **Credit Note** is a Supplier's credit filed into the order's own Document collection, recording which Return to Supplier movements it settles; the reference lives on the document because ledger rows are immutable, and it is per movement so an order that sent several things back and got one credit still shows the rest as **awaiting credit**. A return takes at most one credit note. Credit notes stay on the Purchase Order and are never projected onto linked Jobs the way the order PDF is. The older manually uploaded Job `purchase_order` Document remains supported as historical evidence and is distinct from the live entity.

A new Draft Purchase Order line takes its first unit price from the Part's current moving average, scaled to one standard purchase length for a linear Part, or stays unpriced when the Part has no cost yet. This is an editable default, not a live link: later inventory movements never reprice the line, and changing the draft line's Part replaces the default with that Part's current cost.

**Quote Document** is a generated customer-facing PDF packet owned by a Quote. The newest created Quote Document is treated as the latest. Product Quote packets merge the Product's Brochure at generation time; Custom Quote packets do not include a Product Brochure.

**Brochure** is a generated customer-facing product marketing PDF produced from a Product's Brochure Config, not an uploaded file. It is generated from live config every time: streamed unsaved when viewed from the Product screen, merged into the Quote Document packet at Quote generation, and saved as a standalone Job Document at Job creation. A saved Brochure is immutable and reflects the config as it was at generation time.

**Brochure Config** is the Product-owned configuration the Brochure is generated from: subtitle, key features (an ordered list of freeform lines), and the range logo, hero, technical drawing, and secondary images. The title comes from the Product name, the assembly lists from the Product's Standard and Optional Assemblies, and the body copy from the Product description.

## Access

App Role owns authorization. Department Membership is descriptive only and must not be used to scope permissions.

- **super-admin**: everything admin can do, plus the exclusive ability to review Corrective Feedback and Internal Notes through the Feedback inbox. The only role that can see Corrective Feedback — admins cannot. Only a super-admin may grant or remove the super-admin role; admins manage every other role but cannot mint a super-admin (which would otherwise be an escalation path to Corrective Feedback).
- **admin**: full operational access; owns Bay scheduling, calendar updates, Job creation and updates, admin Bay configuration, Suppliers, Product Unit identity and Ownership Transfers — recording a Transfer by hand is admin-only, since it asserts ownership with no commercial document behind it. Sees General Feedback on subjects it can read, like any subject reader; cannot see Corrective Feedback.
- **procurement-manager**: full Purchase Order access except approval, including posting a PO-bound Return to Supplier; inventory read/adjust and cost read/revalue, but no general inventory move; Customer directory and Product, Part, and Supplier management; Quote create/read/update, but never as the Quote's own salesperson and not cancelling a Locked Quote; Product Unit and Job reads; no scheduling mutation.
- **job-manager**: Job updates, including fabrication timing stamps, plus Job and Product Unit reads; cannot create, schedule, calendar-manage, or cancel Jobs.
- **job-viewer**: Job, Product Unit, and Bay schedule reads only.
- **sales**: Quote create/read/update, Product Unit reads (stock must be selectable on a Quote), and assistant-authored email sending.
- **stores**: Purchase Order read/receive and physical inventory read/move/adjust/count/build/close-out; cannot read inventory costs.
- **bay-operator**: no app permissions and cannot sign in unless the role gains permissions later.

Server/API checks are the security boundary. Browser access checks are UX only.

**Device Account** is a user record for a shared machine rather than a person — today the Stores Tablet. It is a flag on the user (`isDevice`), not a role: its *permissions* are its role's, and what the flag records is that nobody in particular is behind it. Three rules follow, all enforced server-side: a device session must name an actor before it may post any Stock Movement it has the rights to (draws, returns, receipts, supplier returns, adjustments, builds, and close-out — every write the `stores` role can reach), a device may never be named as an actor, and a device has no Badge Card. It is never offered by the Quick-switch. User admin distinguishes a Device Account with a tablet icon and **Device** badge, but follows the reader's selected sorting without pinning devices ahead of people.

**Stores Tablet** is the shared warehouse Device Account running the mobile app, signed in as a single `stores` user. It is the surface for physical stock flows — Checkout, Return to Store, Receipt, Return to Supplier, close-out — while the web app keeps the paperwork. It is price-blind by role, not by screen.

**Quick-switch** is how the Stores Tablet names the person doing the work: a name tap or a **Badge Card** scan sets the actor for the scan session, and an idle timeout clears it. It is not a session and grants nothing — *the device authorizes, the person attributes*. Authorization always comes from the tablet's own session; the quick-switch only decides whose name the Stock Movement's `actor_user_id` records. An actor who is unknown, disabled, or itself a device is refused rather than ignored, and a device that names nobody is refused outright — so a movement never lands under a machine's name. No PIN backs this in v1 — the timeout is the control. Avoid "log in as" for the quick-switch.

**Badge Card** is a printed Code 128 label encoding `badge:<userId>`, scanned at the Stores Tablet's scan field to quick-switch to that person. It identifies; it does not authenticate. Printed from the user screen under `user:set-role`, and never for a Device Account.

## Feedback

**Feedback** is an internal report a signed-in user submits about one subject — currently a Quote or a Job. It always has a submitter (the author) and exactly one subject, attached polymorphically the way a Document is (`subjectType ∈ {quote, job}`). Avoid Complaint, Comment, Review, Ticket, or Issue for the domain object. Visibility splits by Kind: Job General Feedback is public within the workspace (readable by anyone who can read the Job), while Quote General Feedback stays private until quote-scoped read paths exist; Corrective Feedback is readable only by super-admins. Submission requires only an authenticated session: there is no `feedback:create` permission and no subject-read gate, so any signed-in user can submit feedback about any Quote or Job. See ADR 0010.

**Feedback Kind** is exactly one of `general`, `corrective-feedback-department`, or `corrective-feedback-user`. It selects which targets the Feedback carries and who may see it.

**General Feedback** (`general`) is public only when a subject-scoped read/update surface exists. Today that is Job Feedback: anyone who can read the Job can read it with attribution (submitter, date, text), on the Job itself and in the Job Activity feed. Status is carried but never shown on a subject surface — a note read on the shop floor is not a queue to triage — so `feedback.updateJobFeedback` has no caller today. Quote General Feedback remains private until the quote surface gets matching endpoints, and the Feedback inbox is its only reader. General Feedback carries no targets and never exposes Internal Notes.

**Corrective Feedback** is Private Feedback that attributes a problem to one or more **Departments** (`corrective-feedback-department`) or one or more **Users** (`corrective-feedback-user`). Both targets are multi-select. Avoid Blame. Department targets reference the fixed Department enum; User targets reference Users. Only super-admins can read or act on it, through the Feedback inbox; the submission form marks it PRIVATE.

**Feedback Status** is the review lifecycle: `open` (initial), `resolved` (acted on), or `closed` (dismissed). A super-admin moves freely between all three, including reopening. It is a fact about the inbox queue: the Feedback inbox lists and counts Private Feedback only, so the open-Feedback nav indicator (a warning dot on the Feedback menu item) is shown when any *Private* Feedback is `open`. Public Job General Feedback never reaches that queue.

**Internal Notes** is a single mutable free-text field on a Feedback that only super-admins can read or edit.

## Assistant

**Assistant** is the in-app AI chat, enabled per user. It acts strictly as the signed-in user: its capabilities are filtered by that user's permissions, and one capability set applies to the user on every platform — web and mobile run the same Assistant, differing only in presentation. A conversation is ephemeral to the client session and is never shared between devices. Avoid Chatbot, Copilot, or Agent for the domain object.

**App Link** is an internal link the Assistant attaches to an entity it mentions — a Customer, Product, Product Unit, Quote, Job, or downloadable document. App Links use one canonical path shape regardless of platform; each client resolves an activated App Link to its own surface, and a client with no matching surface shows the entity name as plain text rather than a dead or broken link. See ADR 0013.

## Lander Localization

**Locale** is the language of public Lander presentation, identified by code. English (`en`) is the **Canonical Locale**, served at unprefixed URLs; Afrikaans (`af`) is the first translated Locale, served under a locale-prefixed URL tree. The model is locale-keyed so further Locales are additive. See ADR 0011.

**Canonical Text** is the English source text staff author on catalog entities (Products, Ranges, Variants, Assemblies). Slugs, URL parameters, and uniqueness rules derive only from Canonical Text, so URLs are identical across Locales apart from the prefix.

**Translation** is a per-field rendering of an entity's text into a Locale, stored with the entity in an envelope containing its value, Canonical Text source hash, update time, and whether staff own it manually. Every text field is translatable except Model Code. A field is **Stale** when its AI-owned envelope no longer matches Canonical Text; a manual mismatch instead **Needs Review** and never enters the AI queue. Both keep serving, while a missing field falls back to Canonical Text. Saving a manual value stamps the current source hash; reverting it to AI removes the envelope and eagerly queues regeneration. Translation state is derived from stored facts, never tracked as queue state.

**Locale Preference** is a visitor's per-browser remembered Locale. It is detected from the browser language on first visit; an explicit choice via the language dropdown overrides detection, and the Preference wins over the entered URL's Locale on arrival.

## Changelog

**Changelog** is the per-release, user-facing summary of what changed, generated at release time from the released commits, reviewed by the releaser before publishing, and grouped by **Surface** (App, Lander, Mobile). Changes with no user-visible effect are omitted rather than grouped separately. Avoid Release Notes or What's New for the domain object. A Changelog exists only for production releases.

**Changelog Entry** is one titled, described change within a Surface group of a Changelog.

**Changelog View** is a per-user high-water mark: the release time of the newest Changelog the user has acknowledged. A Changelog is **Unseen** for a user when it is newer than their Changelog View, released within the last 30 days, released after the user's account was created, and the environment is production. In the dialog, finishing or skipping advances the Changelog View past everything shown; closing does not.

## Cross-Cutting

**Audit Event** records boundary-visible changes for Customers, Jobs, Job Bays, Product Units, Products, Purchase Orders, Quotes, Suppliers, and Users. Slot create/resize/remove are not audited; Slot reorders are. Feedback is not audited.

**Job Activity** is the cross-Job feed of what has been said and done about Jobs, newest first, read by anyone with `job:read`. An **activity item** is one entry in it, of one of two sorts. The said sort is Job General Feedback, read from `feedback`. The done sort is a **Change Event**, a curated projection of an Audit Event: `job-created`, `job-description-updated`, `job-completed`, and `job-document-added`. A Change Event carries its actor, its Job, and a named payload only — the raw audit change set never reaches the feed, which is what lets both sorts share the `job:read` gate while raw audit reads stay `audit:read` (ADR 0015). Job cancellation, Ownership Transfers, and catalog Optional Assembly changes are not Change Events today. The feed reads existing records and stores no duplicate activity records of its own.

**Activity View** is a per-user high-water mark: the occurrence time of the newest Job Activity item that user has actually received in the unfiltered global feed. An item is **Unread Activity** when it is newer than the user's Activity View. A client advances the mark only to the newest item in the response it displayed; an item arriving between that response and its acknowledgement remains unread.

**Dashboard** is the single signed-in landing surface. Widgets are permission-gated registry entries, and Dashboard Metrics are computed live rather than stored in reporting tables. Its management-only **Inventory KPIs** are cost-gated: Inventory Value is current Stock on Hand at current moving averages, with negative balances subtracting rather than being hidden; Inventory Turns is trailing-90-day stamped net consumption annualized by four and divided by current Perpetual stock value; Top Adjustments groups the current month's absolute adjustment value by reason; and Top Scrap groups the same current-month, `scrap`-reason value by Part. Adjustment and scrap magnitudes are estimates at today's moving average: only Opening Balance adjustments stamp a cost, and the rankings deliberately reprice every reason consistently at the current average. Inventory Turns instead uses the cost stamped on Checkout, Return to Store, and Build consumption movements. Periodic stock is excluded from both sides of Inventory Turns because it records no consumption movements.

**Job Start Alert** is a visibility signal for an accepted Quote with no Job and no allocated Product Unit whose earliest set Preferred/Planned Delivery Date is within two calendar months from the current Johannesburg date. Allocation Quotes are excluded because a stock sale needing no work is complete without a Job. **Awaiting Job Creation** is the complete work queue of accepted Quotes with no live Job and no allocated Product Unit, regardless of whether a delivery date is set or how far away it is; it includes a Quote again when its only Job is cancelled.
