# Product Units Are First-Class, Separate From the Job That Builds Them

The domain conflated the *work of building* (Job) with the *thing built* (the physical machine). A machine's identity — serial, product, VIN — lived on its Job, and its owner was derived through that Job's Quote. That produced a rigid chain: a unit could not exist without a Job, a Job could not exist without a Quote, and a Quote could not exist without a Customer, so **a unit could not exist without a sale**. Building showroom stock therefore required a Customer record standing in for the company itself, and selling that stock had no honest path at all — the real customer's Quote could not attach to the existing Job (`job_quote_id_unique`), so following the normal path scheduled a second build of a machine already standing on the floor. The schema was already asking for the extraction: `job_product_serial_shape` was a five-column all-or-nothing check with a unique index on one of them, which is an entity inlined into a row.

We decided to **extract Product Unit as a first-class entity** owning the serial, product, and VIN, created by its Build Job (0..1 per Job, exactly as the old constraint implied), and to **represent ownership as an append-only Ownership Transfer log** whose newest row's destination is the current Owner — no stored owner column, following the same reasoning that made a Bay's current operator an open assignment interval. A Unit with no Transfers is Stock, meaning we hold it, so "no customer" became a state of the machine instead of a fake Customer row. `job.quoteId` became nullable (`unitId is not null OR quoteId is not null`), which is what actually kills the placeholder customer: a Stock Build is a Job with a **Build Spec** — its own Optional Assembly selection, seeded from a Quote's selections when it has one — and no commercial record until the Unit sells. Selling stock is an **Allocation Quote**: an ordinary Product Quote that references an existing Unit, prices it from the Unit's As-Built Spec as if built to order, locks on acceptance, and sources at most a Rework Job whose CFO covers only the Assemblies being added.

Rejected alternatives: a **transfer Quote kind with a parent Quote pointer** (a new `transferred` status plus `parentQuoteId`) was the original proposal and reads naturally, but it keeps ownership on the Quote — so recording a machine changing hands requires manufacturing a Quote, and a customer-to-customer resale we were not party to would need a fictional price and a salesperson who sold nothing, landing in quote status counts and sales reporting as business we did. It also overwrites a legitimately accepted Quote's status to record a fact about an object in the world. **Keeping the Stock customer** and extracting only the Unit was rejected because it pays most of the migration cost while leaving the fake row in every customer picker — the same failure mode already seen with bar length as a `parts.category` value and in-house manufacture as a *Jedidiah Fabrication* supplier row.

Accepted consequences: current Owner costs a lateral join on every Job and Unit read that shows a customer, where a stored column would not; ownership can move before a machine physically leaves the yard, since Owner means records-attribution rather than legal title; and a cancelled sale leaves two rows (out and back) rather than looking like it never happened, which is what actually occurred. This ADR replaces ADR 0002, whose opening invariant — "A Job is created from exactly one Quote" — this contradicts; the parts of 0002 that still hold (Assemblies and effective BOM, CFO as a frozen bill of materials, Document immutability and Job Document Snapshot, Quote Documents, audit coverage) carry forward unchanged except that a CFO now snapshots from the Job's Build Spec.
