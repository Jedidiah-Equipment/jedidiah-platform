The plan for the contracting app: where it lives, what it records, who touches what, and the order it gets built. Every claim below traces to a resolved decision on the wayfinder map; this page is the walkthrough, the spec issue is the record.

One platform, two businesses ADR 0016

Contracting lives inside the existing system behind a hard wall: its own folders, URLs, and database schema, with nothing crossing to the equipment side. Shared ground holds only what serves both blindly — logins, audit, file storage.

The lifecycle is a chain of queues the core promise

Work used to vanish between a foreman's clipboard and PESTEL. Now every hand-off is a queue somebody watches — a job cannot fall between stages.

No hour can vanish the sequential-hours mechanism

A machine's meter tells one continuous story. The app captures arrival and departure with a photo; the opening of every stint is the previous departure — there is no third number to mistype.

Drivers and mechanics are people in the system without logins — they appear in pickers and history, never on a screen of their own. Business access is simply which role slots a user holds; only both-slot users ever see the mode switcher.

Three stories to walk through

Each one exercises the rules above with the actual people. Follow them on the screens mockup.

1 · Mr Rowley's dam, call to invoicethe happy path

  • Mr Rowley (farmer, the customer) phones Jed (owner): a dam at Rooikraal. Henk (contracting manager) pre-creates job CJOB-00042 — customer, farm Rooikraal, work type Dam building — and assigns Sipho (foreman). It sits in Upcoming; only Sipho sees it.
  • The lowbed drops CAT320-1 (excavator) on site. Sipho assigns it from the Category-filtered picker and photographs the meter; the AI reads 4002.6, one tap confirms — the job flips Active by itself. Trucked in means a zero Hour Gap: no travel line.
  • GRAD140K-1 (grader) drives itself over: arrival 7110.0, 2.1 h past its last departure — under the 4 h threshold, so it quietly becomes a travel line billed to Mr Rowley, the farmer it drove to (the include-travel toggle defaults on).
  • JD6140M-2 (tractor) arrives towing the tip trailer: Sipho assigns it (arrival 7102.5, a 1.2 h drive) and attaches the tip trailer as its Implement.
  • He keys 18 loads hauled on that assignment — production for the record, never billed.
  • The work wraps: Sipho captures each departure — CAT320-1 at 4051.2, GRAD140K-1 at 7152.4, JD6140M-2 at 7141.3. Work hours derive themselves: 48.6, 42.4, 38.8 — nobody typed a total. The job surfaces in Henk's looks-finished queue.
  • Sign-off: every reading photo-backed. Henk adds Charge Lines — lowbed move R 3,500.00, diesel 210 L R 4,830.00 — writes a site note, tweaks the end date, stamps Completed.
  • Jed's pricing queue: rates prefill from Category presets and the lines compute — CAT320-1 48.6 h × R 600 = R 29,160.00; GRAD140K-1 (42.4 + 2.1 travel) 44.5 h × R 550 = R 24,475.00; JD6140M-2 (38.8 + 1.2) 40.0 h × R 450 = R 18,000.00 — the grader rate trimmed as a favour, because the edit is the discount. With Charge Lines: R 79,965.00 ex VAT. Priced — figures freeze.
  • Karen (office — invoicing) opens the Job Card — a works summary, explicitly not an invoice — keys it into PESTEL, stamps INV-4471: Invoiced, the wall.
  • Months later Rowley disputes the hours; Jed prints the customer copy — CAT320-1 arrived at 4002.6, left at 4051.2: there when it got there, there when it left.

2 · The TLB that came homegaps and the yard

  • JCB3CX-1 (TLB) leaves Welgevonden with a departure photo at 6204.4 and, with nothing booked, drives back to Stony Brook. Nobody logs anything — there is no internal job, on purpose. Five weeks of yard digging.
  • Mr Steyn (farmer) wants a TLB at Klipdrift. Henk creates the job, assigns Sipho; the arrival photo reads 6241.8 — 37.4 h past the last departure, way over the 4 h threshold.
  • A Gap Flag rides the assignment. Sipho keys on, unbothered — flags surface at sign-off and never block the field.
  • The hole gets dug; departure 6263.0 — 21.2 work hours, derived.
  • At sign-off Henk splits the gap: 1.6 h billable travel, 35.8 h Unaccounted — "yard work at Stony Brook" — flag cleared. Mr Steyn's card shows 21.2 work hours and a 1.6 h travel line; the yard weeks are his to never see.
  • Had Sipho fat-fingered 6141.8 — below the last reading — the capture would have refused: meters don't run backwards; retake, or assert "the previous reading is wrong," which saves his value as disputed and flags the pair for Henk.

3 · Code Red at Scott Farmthe workshop loop

  • JD6140M-2 pours hydraulic oil at Scott Farm, Willem (driver) at the wheel. Sipho taps Report a problem, holds the mic — a mixed Afrikaans-English voice note. He fixes one mangled word in the transcript (the correction teaches the transcriber), adds two photos, marks it Code Red, sends. Job and farm attach themselves from the open Assignment.
  • Connor (workshop manager) gets the push — Code Red also pings Henk and Jed. A Code Green would have pinged Connor alone.
  • The queue shows the report, photos, and farm — and the derived hint: JD6140M-1 (tractor) on the same job has an open Code Green (brake light). One trip, two fixes. Connor assigns Danie (mechanic), marks In Progress.
  • Connor drops a question into the Breakdown Notes thread — "which side is the leak?" — and Sipho answers by voice note, transcribed. The back-and-forth lives on the Breakdown, not in a WhatsApp scroll.
  • Danie replaces the burst pipe, sorts the brake light while he's there. Connor closes both with close-out notes; each lands permanently on its machine's history.
  • The tap-to-close clock becomes Danie's number on the mechanic performance tab: 0.8 days, no comebacks.
  • While Danie works, Sipho snaps a spot reading — 7148.9, 51 h from the next service (mechanics have no login; field captures are the foreman's). The profile turns amber — Service due soon — and Connor gets that ping too. Filters are ordered before anyone asks.

Build order

  1. Phase 0 — set the symmetric standard. Move the existing equipment app into its namespace (folders, URLs with redirects, database schema, permission names) before any contracting code lands.
  2. Fleet & hours foundation. Machines, categories, readings with photo capture, the one-time fleet seed with baseline hours.
  3. Job lifecycle. Jobs, assignments, the queues, sign-off with gap resolution, pricing, invoicing, the Job Card PDF.
  4. Workshop. Breakdowns with voice notes and push notifications, servicing with due-soon tracking.
  5. Reporting. Days-based utilisation with the target line; mechanic performance.

Deliberately out of v1

· Planning/forecasting — the Google Sheet stays
· A bespoke notification center (read receipts, threads) — real OS push notifications are in v1: breakdowns and service-due warnings ping the right phones
· PESTEL integration — Karen keys from the Job Card
· WhatsApp delivery — shelved fallback
· Internal/yard jobs — resolved gaps with reasons instead
· Per-machine rate overrides — the category rate is the rule
· Hour-based utilisation — days are the v1 metric
· Driver-history reporting beyond assignment snapshots
· Team attendance register — a later idea from Jed's brief
